Notifications, where you're looking.

On a 32:9 ultrawide, macOS notifications always land in the far top-right corner, where they're easy to miss. GazeNotification reads the direction of your face and eyes through your webcam and moves notifications above wherever you're looking.

How it works

  1. The camera looks at your face

    A few times a second, Vision finds your face, eyes, nose, and pupils on the Neural Engine.

  2. It works out where across the screen you're looking

    It combines your head direction and pupil position to estimate which part of the screen you're looking at. A one-time 15-second calibration makes it accurate.

  3. It moves the notification spot ahead of time

    Before a notification arrives, the spot where it will appear is already moved to where you're looking, so it shows up there from the start. If your gaze moves while it's on screen, it follows.

  4. It puts things back when needed

    When you open Notification Center or quit the app, the notification window goes back to its original place.

Battery, privacy, accuracy

Easy on the battery
It switches to Balanced on a power adapter and to Power Saving on battery, in Low Power Mode, or when the Mac runs hot. It computes less while your gaze rests or you're away, and turns the camera off when the screen locks. On an M4 Max the whole app used about 3% CPU in Power Saving and about 7% in Balanced.
Video never leaves your Mac
Camera frames are turned into a few numbers describing your face direction in memory and discarded right away. No video or photos are saved, and the app has no networking at all.
Tune it yourself
On top of the calibration you can adjust the horizontal shift and range, per-feature weights, jitter suppression, and screen zones by hand. It automatically picks the lowest-error combination of three face analysis methods and three estimation models.
See what it computes
Graphs of CPU usage, processing rate, and time per run over the last 10 minutes, with your own limits for each situation and a CPU cap.
Four languages
한국어, English, 日本語, 简体中文. Switch languages from the app's menu.

Install

  1. Open the DMG and drag GazeNotification into the Applications folder.

  2. Open GazeNotification. An eye icon appears in the menu bar instead of the Dock.

    This version is a preview that hasn't been notarized by Apple yet. If macOS says it can't verify the app the first time you open it, go to System Settings → Privacy & Security and click “Open Anyway” at the bottom. After updating to a new version you may need to turn Accessibility access back on.

  3. Allow Accessibility and Camera access. Accessibility is used to move the notification window, and the camera to read your gaze.

  4. Choose “Calibrate Gaze” in the menu and look at the 5 dots that appear one by one along the top of the screen. It takes about 15 seconds.

You'll need macOS 14 or later and a webcam centered on top of your monitor (a built-in camera or an iPhone via Continuity Camera also works).

Questions

Why isn't it on the Mac App Store?

Mac App Store apps must run in the App Sandbox, and sandboxed apps can't use the Accessibility features that move other apps' windows. Notification banners belong to macOS's Notification Center, so they can't be moved from inside the sandbox.

Is the camera always on?

It's on while tracking your gaze, and the camera indicator light is on too. It turns off when the display sleeps or the screen locks, and after a long absence it turns off until you use the keyboard or mouse again. If you set the position source to the mouse, the camera isn't used at all.

Does it work on Intel Macs?

Yes. It's a universal app for Apple Silicon and Intel. Macs without a Neural Engine run the models on the GPU, which uses a little more CPU.

What about multiple displays?

For now it estimates where you're looking across the main display only, and notifications appear along the top of the main display.

Notifications appear in an odd place after the app was force quit.

If the app quits unexpectedly, the notification window can stay where it was moved. Open the app again, or run killall NotificationCenter in Terminal to put it back.

Privacy policy

Effective October 3, 2026

GazeNotification doesn't collect personal data. The app doesn't communicate with any server.

Camera video is processed only in your Mac's memory. Each frame is turned into a few numbers describing your face direction and then discarded right away. No video or photos are saved or sent anywhere.

This Mac stores your settings, the calibration result (face feature numbers and screen positions), and diagnostic logs (~/Library/Logs/GazeNotification). Logs contain only the structure and text lengths of the notification window, never notification content. To remove everything, delete the app, run defaults delete party.udon.GazeNotification in Terminal, and delete the log folder.
